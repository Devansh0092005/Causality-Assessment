# Causality Assessment
## Case Study

A 55-year-old patient with hypertension was started on **amlodipine 5 mg once daily**. Approximately two weeks later, the patient developed **swelling of both ankles and feet**. The patient had no previous history of significant leg swelling. After consultation with the doctor, amlodipine was discontinued and an alternative antihypertensive medication was prescribed. The swelling gradually improved.

## Formal Causality Assessment Table

| Assessment Criterion      | Findings                                                                  | Assessment              | Overall Assessment                     |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **Temporal relationship** | Reaction developed approximately two weeks after starting amlodipine.     | Appropriate             | Supports a drug-related reaction       |
| **Known association**     | Peripheral edema is a recognized adverse effect of amlodipine.            | Present                 | Supports causality                     |
| **Dechallenge**           | Swelling gradually improved after amlodipine was discontinued.            | Positive                | Strongly supports causality            |
| **Rechallenge**           | Amlodipine was not restarted.                                             | Not performed           | Does not confirm recurrence            |
| **Previous history**      | No significant leg swelling was present before treatment.                 | Absent                  | Supports causality                     |
| **Alternative causes**    | Cardiac, renal, hepatic, or venous conditions may cause peripheral edema. | Not completely excluded | Causality cannot be considered certain |
| **Dose relationship**     | No dose increase or dose-response information is available.               | Not assessable          | No additional support available        |
| **Clinical outcome**      | Swelling gradually improved after withdrawal of amlodipine.               | Improved                | Supports a positive dechallenge        |

## Final Causality Assessment

| Classification | Overall Assessment                                                                                                                 |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Certain**    | Not established because rechallenge was not performed and alternative causes cannot be completely excluded.                        |
| **Probable**   | **Established as the most appropriate classification based on the available case information.**                                    |
| **Possible**   | Less consistent with the available evidence because the temporal relationship and positive dechallenge provide additional support. |

### Final Assessment

**Causality Classification: PROBABLE**

The patient's peripheral edema is **probably related to amlodipine**, based on the appropriate temporal relationship, recognized adverse-effect profile, absence of significant prior edema, and positive dechallenge.
